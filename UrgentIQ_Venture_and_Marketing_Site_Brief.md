# UrgentIQ --- Venture Overview & Marketing Website Brief

## 1. Venture at a Glance

**Name:** UrgentIQ\
**Category:** Healthcare technology / Deeptech\
**Primary market:** Hospitals and emergency-care organisations\
**Product:** AI-assisted dynamic triage and patient-prioritisation
platform\
**Delivery model:** Cloud-based platform with a live dashboard\
**Business model direction:** B2B, with hospital-level subscription
pricing

**One-line description:**\
UrgentIQ is an AI-assisted dynamic triage platform designed to help
emergency departments identify patients whose condition may be
deteriorating, surface changing risk, and keep patient priority visible
to clinical staff.

## 2. The Problem

Emergency departments operate under pressure: patient volumes fluctuate,
staff attention is limited, and patient conditions can change while they
wait.

A triage assessment is often a snapshot taken at a particular moment. A
patient initially assessed as lower priority may deteriorate before the
next reassessment. Manual observation and periodic vital-sign checks can
leave gaps between assessments, especially in busy emergency
departments.

This creates three connected challenges:

-   **For patients:** deterioration may not be recognised promptly.
-   **For clinical teams:** monitoring many waiting patients adds
    workload and cognitive pressure.
-   **For hospitals:** limited visibility into changing patient risk can
    make prioritisation and resource allocation harder.

## 3. The UrgentIQ Approach

UrgentIQ is designed as a continuous clinical decision-support layer
between initial triage and definitive treatment.

The platform's intended workflow is:

1.  Relevant patient information, such as vitals and symptoms, enters
    the system.
2.  The platform processes incoming information and trends over time.
3.  Risk-analysis logic identifies patterns or changes that may indicate
    deterioration.
4.  The system updates a patient risk indicator or score.
5.  The dashboard surfaces patients whose risk appears to be increasing.
6.  Clinical staff receive a visual alert or prioritisation cue.
7.  A qualified clinician reviews the information and decides whether
    reassessment or intervention is required.

**Core idea:** Initial triage creates a snapshot. UrgentIQ is designed
to keep patient priority responsive to new information.

## 4. Product Capabilities

The venture's intended product capabilities include:

-   **Patient monitoring view:** A consolidated view of relevant patient
    information.
-   **Deterioration indicators:** Highlighting changes that may warrant
    clinical attention.
-   **Dynamic risk scoring:** Updating risk indicators as new data
    becomes available.
-   **Priority queue:** Surfacing patients whose risk is increasing.
-   **Staff alerts:** Drawing attention to patients who may need
    reassessment.
-   **Trend analysis:** Considering changes over time, not only isolated
    readings.
-   **Integration direction:** Designed to connect with hospital
    information and clinical systems through appropriate APIs and
    integrations.

These are product goals and intended capabilities. A marketing website
must distinguish implemented prototype features from planned or future
capabilities.

## 5. Who It Is For

### Primary customers

Medium-to-large urban hospitals with emergency departments and
moderate-to-high technology adoption.

### Secondary customers

-   Private hospitals
-   Emergency-care centres
-   Hospital groups evaluating emergency-department workflow technology

### Key stakeholders and users

-   Emergency physicians
-   Nurses and triage staff
-   Emergency-department managers
-   Clinical operations leaders
-   Hospital administrators and technology decision-makers

The website should speak to both clinical users and hospital
decision-makers: clinical teams need useful, clear signals;
administrators need workflow visibility, operational value, and a
credible implementation path.

## 6. Value Proposition

UrgentIQ aims to help emergency departments:

-   Identify potentially deteriorating patients sooner.
-   Maintain a more current view of patient priority.
-   Reduce dependence on memory and fragmented manual observation.
-   Direct staff attention toward patients whose condition may be
    changing.
-   Support more informed reassessment and resource allocation.
-   Improve visibility across the emergency-department waiting workflow.

Do not promise guaranteed reductions in mortality, waiting times, costs,
or adverse events unless supported by appropriate clinical evidence.

## 7. Positioning and Differentiation

UrgentIQ should not be marketed merely as "AI for healthcare." Its
clearest positioning is:

**A continuous patient-prioritisation layer for emergency departments.**

The differentiating concept is the transition from one-time triage to a
continuously updated view of changing patient risk.

A concise positioning line:

> Existing systems collect clinical information. UrgentIQ is designed to
> turn changing patient information into a continuously updated view of
> who may need attention next.

This is a product-positioning statement, not a claim that UrgentIQ
replaces existing hospital systems.

## 8. Technology Direction

The technical direction includes:

-   Artificial intelligence and machine learning
-   Predictive analytics
-   Real-time data processing
-   Cloud infrastructure
-   APIs and system integrations
-   Clinical decision-support algorithms
-   A live web dashboard

Conceptual data flow:

`Patient Data → Data Processing → Risk Analysis → Deterioration Indicators → Dynamic Risk View → Priority Dashboard / Alert → Clinical Review`

The website should communicate the outcome and workflow rather than
overwhelm visitors with technical jargon.

## 10. Current Stage and Prototype Context

UrgentIQ has been explored through a web dashboard concept and a
smartwatch-vitals demonstration workflow. The prototype work includes
recorded or test vitals and a five-minute baseline-recording experience.

The prototype is intended to demonstrate the product concept and data
flow. It must not be represented as a certified medical device,
clinically validated diagnostic tool, or production-ready hospital
deployment unless those statuses are independently established.

For public marketing, describe the current state accurately: - Use
"designed to," "aims to," "prototype," or "in development" where
appropriate. - Label simulated, recorded, or test data clearly in any
demo. - Do not imply that a test reading is a real patient reading. - Do
not imply that the smartwatch alone provides a clinically reliable
assessment. - Do not imply autonomous diagnosis or treatment
recommendations.

## 11. Clinical Safety and Trust Principles

UrgentIQ is intended to support, not replace, clinical judgement.

The marketing website should clearly communicate:

-   UrgentIQ is a decision-support concept/platform.
-   Clinical professionals remain responsible for assessment and care
    decisions.
-   Alerts are prompts for review, not diagnoses.
-   Product capabilities and performance claims require validation.
-   Patient data must be handled securely and in accordance with
    applicable privacy, security, and healthcare requirements.
-   No real patient information should appear in public demos or
    screenshots.

Avoid unsupported claims such as: - "Prevents deaths" - "Eliminates
triage errors" - "Guarantees faster treatment" - "100% accurate AI" -
"Replaces nurses or doctors" - "Clinically proven" unless evidence and
approval exist

## 12. Marketing Website Objective

The marketing website is a public-facing introduction to UrgentIQ. It is
separate from the existing authenticated dashboard.

Its job is to: 1. Explain the emergency-department problem. 2. Introduce
UrgentIQ's approach. 3. Show the product workflow and dashboard preview.
4. Explain benefits for clinical teams and hospitals. 5. Establish
credibility without overstating validation. 6. Encourage hospitals and
stakeholders to request a demo or contact the team. 7. Provide a clear
**Login** path to the existing dashboard.

The marketing site should not rebuild or replace the dashboard.

## 14. Brand and Visual Direction

The marketing site must inherit the visual language of the existing
UrgentIQ dashboard.

Before coding: - Inspect the current repository and identify the
existing dashboard's colours, typography, spacing, border radius,
shadows, icon style, button treatments, and component patterns. - Reuse
existing design tokens and components wherever practical. - Use the
existing logo and assets if present. - Do not introduce an unrelated
visual identity.

The intended feel is: - Modern healthcare technology - Calm, precise,
and trustworthy - Clean and professional - Data-aware without looking
like a dense analytics dashboard - Clear and accessible for busy
healthcare professionals

Avoid: - Generic hospital stock-photo aesthetics - Excessive gradients
and glowing effects - Overly playful illustrations - Dense walls of
text - Fake live patient data - Unverifiable medical claims

## 15. Conversion and Navigation Behaviour

-   **Login** must route to the existing dashboard's current login
    route.
-   If the application already has authentication and route guards,
    preserve them.
-   If a visitor is already authenticated, the login action may route
    directly to the dashboard.
-   **Request a Demo** should open a working contact form or use an
    existing contact workflow. If backend submission is not configured,
    clearly mark the form as a frontend placeholder and do not show a
    false "message sent" success state.
-   Keep marketing pages public.
-   Keep dashboard routes protected.
-   Do not break existing dashboard URLs, APIs, authentication, or data
    models.

# Appendix: Implementation Guardrails

1.  Treat the existing dashboard as the source of truth for visual
    design and authentication.
2.  Build the marketing site around it; do not redesign the dashboard.
3.  Reuse the current stack, routing, styles, and shared components
    where possible.
4.  Avoid unnecessary dependencies.
5.  Do not hardcode secrets or expose API keys.
6.  Do not use real patient information in sample content.
7.  Make the site responsive and accessible.
8.  Test public routes, login navigation, protected dashboard routes,
    and existing functionality.
9.  Clearly label unfinished integrations and placeholder content.
10. Before finishing, provide a concise summary of files changed, routes
    added, and tests performed.
